\abstract{
We study protocol integrity and rule-weight masking for a TSK scoring engine. The analytical variance identity shows that injected output variance is the realised noise variance $\sigma_\xi^2$ times a row-specific consequent-dispersion scale $S_t$; effective rule count is only an upper-bound diagnostic. A truncation-calibrated dispersion-adaptive mask reaches a target noise floor when the non-negativity cap permits, but remains defense-in-depth. We evaluate seven Kazakhstan Stock Exchange equities with a pre-2025 frozen specification: the 36-point hyperparameter scan uses an early/late chronological split before 2025, but does not purge the fit--validation boundary, so the selection step is not fully leakage-free. A separate 30-trading-day embargo removes labels whose forward windows cross the 2024--2025 test boundary. Under this conditional frozen specification, the 2025 monthly cross-sectional Rank IC is $0.201$ (date-clustered 95\% CI $[-0.013,0.432]$), the 15-bps top-3 Sharpe is $0.98$, and the 150-bps Sharpe is $0.74$. The frozen-model masking audit gives single-seed in-window SVR fidelity $R^2=0.533$ without masking and $0.377$ with dispersion-adaptive masking at $\epsilon=2.0$; keyed repeated-query fidelity is $0.453$ for both $Q=1$ and $Q=25$. These are conditional diagnostics, not a robust security guarantee. The previously deployed, test-informed specification is reported only as a sensitivity. The trial ledger does not contain the per-trial portfolio returns required for a proper deflated-Sharpe calculation, so no 36-trial DSR is claimed. These results do not establish monthly ranking skill or a robust security benefit; they document a negative result with an explicitly qualified selection protocol and quantify a limited masking noise trade-off.
}

\keywords{Protocol Integrity, TSK Fuzzy Inference, ANFIS, Output Masking, Model Extraction, Emerging Markets}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec1}

An algorithmic scoring engine can expose proprietary rules through repeated prediction queries. This makes the engine an attack surface for model extraction, independently of whether its scores have trading value. We therefore frame the study as an intellectual-property and protocol-integrity audit, not as a front-running or payout-prediction claim. The security question is narrow and testable: can output noise measurably degrade reconstruction under a stated query and observability model?

Our answer begins with an analytical result. Proposition~\ref{thm:masking} shows that the variance injected by rule-weight masking equals a weighted consequent-dispersion scale $S_t$ multiplied by the noise variance $\sigma_\xi^2$: the effective number of active rules enters only as an \emph{upper bound} on that variance, so rule concentration alone neither proves nor guarantees suppression --- efficacy at any row is governed by $S_t$ and the noise scale. On the frozen panel, subtractive clustering produces concentrated firing (median $K_{\mathrm{eff}} = 1.40$ of $K = 3$). Inverting the variance identity per observation, with the draw's truncation calibrated out, yields a dispersion-adaptive scheme, but the non-negativity cap binds on $77.2\%$ of rows at $\epsilon=2.0$, so the target is often unreachable. The resulting reconstruction reduction is conditional and modest: the seeded in-window SVR fidelity changes from $0.533$ unmasked to $0.377$ under adaptive masking, while keyed repeated-query fidelity remains $0.453$ for $Q=1$ and $Q=25$.

We validate the masking claim on a four-year Kazakhstan Stock Exchange panel (2022--2024 training, 2025 strictly out-of-sample), scoring every adversary against the unmasked scores. The full audit uses the frozen $K=3$, radius-$0.4$, ridge-$100$ model: in the single seeded in-window SVR audit, unmasked fidelity is $R^2=0.533$, fixed $\alpha=0.05$ gives $0.530$, and dispersion-adaptive masking at $\epsilon=2.0$ gives $0.377$; keyed masking gives $0.453$ and is unchanged when the same keyed responses are repeated $25$ times. These are conditional single-panel, single-seed diagnostics rather than security guarantees. The frozen model's median $K_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is $1.40$ and its adaptive cap-binding rate is $77.2\%$; the earlier deployed $K=5$ audit is retained only as a sensitivity and is not used for the primary claim. The remaining claims are therefore limited to a variance identity, its truncation-calibrated inversion, and a negative security audit under explicit observability assumptions. The architecture is illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}.

The main contributions of this study are twofold:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Analytical masking audit:} A truncation-calibrated variance identity and its row-wise inversion, with explicit separation of systematic bias from injected score noise and a negative audit against query averaging and observable out-of-window history.
    \item \textbf{Reproducible evaluation protocol:} A pre-2025 hyperparameter ledger, a 30-trading-day label embargo, date-clustered uncertainty intervals, and cost sensitivity through 150 bps. No contribution is claimed for predictive finance performance.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec2}

\subsection{Fuzzy Inference Systems in Quantitative Finance}

Fuzzy logic systems, originally introduced by Zadeh \cite{zadeh1965} and expanded into functional inference by Takagi and Sugeno \cite{takagi1985}, have seen extensive application in financial time-series forecasting due to their ability to model non-linear boundaries with human-interpretable linguistic rules. The TSK+ paper cited here concerns sparse-rule fuzzy interpolation and rule-base generation, not a general claim of numerical stability on noisy financial data \cite{li2018}. Rubio et al.\ \cite{rubio2021} report ANFIS forecasting with noise-robustness analysis, while the evolving-TSK survey of Skrjanc et al.\ \cite{skrjanc2019} reviews online rule adaptation. Within this family, ANFIS remains a standard optimization framework because it combines interpretable local consequents with least-squares and gradient-based learning \cite{jang1993}.

\subsection{Market Microstructure Proxies in Emerging Markets}

In market microstructure research, Cont et al. \cite{cont2014} established the predictive power of Order Flow Imbalance ($\mathrm{OFI}$) for short-term price dynamics. True $\mathrm{OFI}$ requires event-level bid/ask volumes and is unavailable in most emerging-market daily datasets. We therefore use the classical close-location value (CLV), the relative position of the close within the daily high--low range, as a low-frequency range feature; it is not an independent order-flow measure and no order-flow validation is claimed. Corwin and Schultz \cite{corwin2012} derive bid-ask spread estimators from daily ranges, and Fong et al.\ \cite{fong2017} benchmark low-frequency liquidity proxies against high-frequency standards. Chordia et al.\ \cite{chordia2001} and Fama and French \cite{fama2008} motivate liquidity-aware controls more generally, but do not establish CLV as a directional-pressure statistic. Where intraday quotes are unavailable, a simple tick rule $\mathrm{sgn}(P_t - P_{t-1})$ is used as a fallback; it is correlated with CLV because both depend on the close and is not treated as an independent validation signal. The tick rule also differs from the classical Lee--Ready algorithm \cite{leeready1991}, which requires intraday quote midpoints.

\subsection{Adversarial Robustness and Algorithmic Trading Security}

As algorithmic trading systems become more transparent, they become vulnerable to reverse engineering and predatory strategies. Easley et al. \cite{easley2012} show that high-frequency order flow can create toxicity and liquidity fragility, while Cartea et al. \cite{cartea2015} provide a comprehensive treatment of optimal execution and strategic interaction in algorithmic markets.

The proposed mechanism sits beside, rather than replaces, model-extraction defenses. Tram{\`e}r et al.\ \cite{tramer2016} and Orekondy et al.\ \cite{orekondy2019} study extraction attacks; prediction poisoning \cite{orekondy2020} and deceptive perturbations \cite{lee2019} directly perturb outputs or predictions to frustrate stealing; and PRADA detects suspicious query distributions \cite{juuti2019}. Randomized smoothing \cite{cohen2019} and the Gaussian mechanism \cite{dwork2006} are methodological analogies, not model-extraction defenses. Our narrower contribution is a row-wise variance/bias accounting for perturbing internal TSK rule weights during a stated event-window policy. The audit finds limited friction against a weak in-window extractor, not a durable defense, and evaluates query averaging explicitly in Section~\ref{subsec4_4}.

\section{Proposed Methodology}\label{sec3}

\subsection{Preprocessing and Feature Extraction}\label{subsec3_1}

To completely eliminate lookahead bias, all features computed at time $t$ rely strictly on the historical trailing window $[t-N, t-1]$. In the canonical three-input baseline used throughout the frozen protocol, $N=20$ trading days for both the VWAP and volume transforms (Tier-2 additionally reports a separate long-horizon column with $N=60$). Define the trailing volume set
\[
V_N(t) := \{V_{t-1},V_{t-2},\ldots,V_{t-N}\}.
\]
The historical $\mathrm{VWAP}$ available at time $t$ is computed over $N$ days excluding day $t$:

\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{VWAP}_{t-1}(N) = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N} P_{t-i} \cdot V_{t-i}}{\sum_{i=1}^{N} V_{t-i}},
    \label{eq:vwap}
\end{equation}
where $P_{t-i}$ and $V_{t-i}$ denote the closing price and trading volume on day $t-i$. Eq.~\eqref{eq:vwap} represents the volume-weighted average of closing prices over the trailing $N$-day period.

A regularization threshold $\epsilon_{\mathrm{reg},t} > 0$ is defined row-wise as $\epsilon_{\mathrm{reg},t} = 10^{-6} \cdot \mathrm{Mean}(V_N(t))$. The relative price deviation percentage at time $t$ is:

\begin{equation}
    Dev_{\mathrm{Price},t} = \left( \frac{P_t - \mathrm{VWAP}_{t-1}(N)}{\mathrm{VWAP}_{t-1}(N)} \right) \times 100\%.
    \label{eq:devprice}
\end{equation}

The volume dynamics are captured via a logarithmic relative transformation:

\begin{equation}
    Dev_{\mathrm{Volume},t} = \ln \left( \frac{V_t + \epsilon_{\mathrm{reg},t}}{\mathrm{Median}(V_N(t)) + \epsilon_{\mathrm{reg},t}} \right),
    \label{eq:devvol}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{Median}(V_N(t))$ is the rolling median volume over the trailing window $[t-N, t-1]$.

To capture directional pressure without Level-1 feeds, we use a \emph{close-location value} (CLV) statistic:

\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{CLV}_t = \frac{2 P_t - H_t - L_t}{H_t - L_t + \epsilon_{\mathrm{reg}}}, \quad \mathrm{CLV}_t \in [-1, 1],
    \label{eq:clv}
\end{equation}
where $H_t$, $L_t$, and $P_t$ are the daily high, low, and close, and $\epsilon_{\mathrm{reg}}=10^{-6}$ is a numerical denominator guard for zero-range bars. $\mathrm{CLV}_t$ measures where the close sits inside the daily range; positive values indicate upward pressure, negative values downward. No Level-1 order-flow feed exists for these KASE names, so a direct CLV-versus-true-OFI correlation is infeasible. As an internal-consistency audit instead (\texttt{experiments/ofi\_proxy\_audit.py}, $5{,}993$ asset-days 2022--2025), the CLV proxy has a next-day signed-return Rank IC of $-0.111$ (negative at this horizon, so it carries no standalone next-day sign information), while the two independent daily directional readings coincide: CLV-versus-tick-rule Spearman $\rho = 0.499$ with $69.9\%$ sign agreement. The proxy is therefore reported as directionally coherent but unvalidated against true order flow, and the portfolio results never rely on its next-day sign.

The system selects the directional input via \texttt{clv\_mode}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{CLV} (\texttt{clv\_mode='clv'}): Eq.~\eqref{eq:clv} using daily price bounds.
    \item \textbf{Tick rule} (\texttt{clv\_mode='tick'}): $\mathrm{sgn}(P_t - P_{t-1})$ when daily bounds are unreliable.
    \item \textbf{Hybrid} (\texttt{clv\_mode='hybrid'}): CLV if $\{H_t, L_t\}$ exist and $H_t > L_t$, otherwise tick rule fallback.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Tier-2 Feature Enrichment}\label{subsubsec3_1_2}

The canonical vector $\mathbf{x}_t = [Dev_{\mathrm{Price},t}, Dev_{\mathrm{Volume},t}, \mathrm{CLV}_t]^T$ is extended with two optional blocks, each following the same strictly trailing convention as Eqs.~\eqref{eq:vwap}--\eqref{eq:clv}. The \emph{multi-scale microstructure} block adds five columns: a longer-horizon VWAP deviation $Dev_{\mathrm{Price},60,t}$ computed from Eq.~\eqref{eq:devprice} with $N = 60$; lagged short-term momentum $Ret_{5,t} = (P_{t-1}/P_{t-6} - 1)\times 100$; trailing realised volatility $\sigma_{20,t} = \sqrt{\mathrm{Var}_{[t-21,\,t-2]}(r)}\times 100$ of daily returns; a smoothed directional-pressure statistic $\overline{\mathrm{CLV}}_{5,t} = \frac{1}{5}\sum_{j=1}^{5} \mathrm{CLV}_{t-j}$; and a rolling Amihud illiquidity measure~\cite{amihud2002}
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{Amihud}_{20,t} = 10^{6}\cdot\frac{1}{20}\sum_{j=1}^{20} \frac{|r_{t-j}|}{P_{t-j} V_{t-j}},
    \label{eq:amihud}
\end{equation}
so that every Tier-2 column at time $t$ is a function only of bars in $[t-60,\, t]$. The \emph{slow-fundamental} block attaches net-debt-to-EBITDA leverage, FCFE-to-EBITDA coverage, and annual EBITDA growth per asset, each carried forward point-in-time by an as-of join under a conservative reporting lag $L_{\mathrm{rep}} = 90$ calendar days after period end, so no day observes figures that were not yet public. Because Tier-2 columns span several orders of magnitude (e.g., $\mathrm{Amihud}_{20,t}$ in Table~\ref{tab:tier2_features} has a training-range width below $10^{-4}$ while $Dev_{\mathrm{Price},60,t}$ spans roughly $21\%$), subtractive clustering and Gaussian widths in Eq.~\eqref{eq:gauss_mf} would otherwise be dominated by the widest input; Tier-2 variants therefore pass through a train-fitted min-max transform to $[0,1]$ before entering the inference engine. All scaling constants are estimated on the training fold only and frozen for evaluation, preserving the lookahead-free property of Section~\ref{subsec3_1}.

\subsection{First-Order Takagi--Sugeno--Kang (TSK) Inference Engine}\label{subsec3_2}

The engine maps the market vector $\mathbf{x}_t = [Dev_{\mathrm{Price},t}, Dev_{\mathrm{Volume},t}, \mathrm{CLV}_t]^T \in \mathbb{R}^3$ to a scalar equity attractiveness score $Y_{\mathrm{attr},t} \in [0, 100]$.

Let $K$ denote the number of fuzzy rules derived via subtractive clustering. The
primary specification is frozen before observing 2025 at $K=3$, influence radius
$r=0.4$, and ridge penalty $\lambda=100$. The 36-point pre-2025 grid was
$K\in\{3,5,7\}$, $r\in\{0.3,0.4,0.5,0.6\}$, and
$\lambda\in\{1,10,100\}$; candidates were fit on the early half of the
2022--2024 training window and validated on its late half, selecting maximum
validation Rank IC subject to empirical $C\leq100$. This internal split is
chronological and strictly pre-2025, but it does not purge the final 30
trading days of the fit half or impose an embargo before validation; the
selected hyperparameters can therefore be optimistically affected by
overlapping forward labels. Inputs are kept on the
canonical raw scale (the optional min--max transform is not used). The frozen
choice has 21 free consequent/width parameters; $K=3$ is the rule count and is
unrelated to the separate top-three portfolio size. Rule $k$ ($k = 1,\dots,K$) is formulated as:

\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
    \text{Rule } k: \;& \text{IF } Dev_{\mathrm{Price},t} \text{ IS } A_k \text{ AND } Dev_{\mathrm{Volume},t} \text{ IS } B_k \text{ AND } \mathrm{CLV}_t \text{ IS } C_k \\
    & \text{THEN } y_{k,t} = \beta_{0,k} + \beta_{1,k} Dev_{\mathrm{Price},t} + \beta_{2,k} Dev_{\mathrm{Volume},t} + \beta_{3,k} \mathrm{CLV}_t,
    \end{split}
    \label{eq:tsk_rule}
\end{equation}
where $A_k, B_k, C_k$ are Gaussian membership functions with parameters $\{c_{i,k}, \sigma_{i,k}\}_{i=1}^3$ and $\boldsymbol{\beta}_k = [\beta_{0,k}, \beta_{1,k}, \beta_{2,k}, \beta_{3,k}]^T$.

The membership degree for input $i$ under rule $k$ is:

\begin{equation}
    \mu_{i,k}(x_{i,t}) = \exp \left( -\frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{x_{i,t} - c_{i,k}}{\max(\sigma_{i,k}, \delta)} \right)^2 \right),
    \label{eq:gauss_mf}
\end{equation}
with $\delta = 10^{-4}$ as a numerical floor. The firing strength is:

\begin{equation}
    w_{k,t} = \prod_{i=1}^{3} \mu_{i,k}(x_{i,t}).
    \label{eq:rule_firing}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Defuzzification and Output Clipping}\label{subsec3_3}

The unconstrained crisp output follows from weighted-average defuzzification:

\begin{equation}
    Y_{\mathrm{raw},t} = \frac{\sum_{k=1}^{K} w_{k,t} \, y_{k,t}}{\sum_{k=1}^{K} w_{k,t}} = \sum_{k=1}^{K} \bar{w}_{k,t} \, y_{k,t},
    \label{eq:defuzz_raw}
\end{equation}
where $\bar{w}_{k,t} = w_{k,t} / \sum_{j=1}^{K} w_{j,t}$. If $\sum_{k=1}^{K} w_{k,t} < 10^{-8}$, the system returns the neutral default $Y_{\mathrm{raw},t} = 50.0$.

The output is bounded to $[0,100]$ by projection:

\begin{equation}
    Y_{\mathrm{attr},t} = \mathrm{clamp}(Y_{\mathrm{raw},t}, 0, 100) = \max\bigl(0, \min(100, Y_{\mathrm{raw},t})\bigr).
    \label{eq:clipping}
\end{equation}

On the frozen validation panel, the unclamped empirical envelope
$C=\max_{t,k}|y_{k,t}-Y_{\mathrm{raw},t}|$ for the selected trial is $59.01$
points; the reported score still applies the final
$[0,100]$ projection. The masking experiments below use the separately labelled
deployed model and its own envelope, rather than silently transferring this
number.

\subsubsection{ANFIS Hybrid Training Protocol}\label{subsubsec3_3_1}

The available daily bar history covers 5 January 2022 to 31 December 2025 ($6{,}140$ bars in total: $983$--$984$ for KCEL, KEGC, KZAP, KZTK and KZTO, $756$ for KMGZ which lists from 9 December 2022, and $467$ for AIRA from 12 February 2024). The primary experiment uses the 2022--2024 calendar window as a pooled training panel ($4{,}278$ asset-days over $718$ trading days), removes the final 30 trading days whose forward labels cross the 2025 boundary ($4{,}061$ rows remain), and evaluates strictly out-of-sample on 2025. The deployed $K=5,r=0.5,\lambda=10$ model is retained only as a labelled sensitivity in masking analyses; no recalibration is performed during 2025.

The forward 30-day risk-adjusted return is defined as:

\begin{equation}
    R_{30,t} = \frac{P_{t+30} + \sum d_{t,t+30} - P_t}{P_t} - r^{(30)}_{f,t},
    \label{eq:fwd_return}
\end{equation}
where $\sum d_{t,t+30}$ denotes dividends paid over $(t, t+30]$ and $r^{(30)}_{f,t}$ is the cumulative 30-day rate implied by compounding daily TONIA repo yields. The supplied dividends file is loaded point-in-time: cancelled declarations are excluded and paid dividends are added to $R_{30}$, so the reported label is dividend-adjusted rather than price-only. The KZTK 2025-05-25 field is a Sunday record date and is not treated as a verified exchange ex-date.

The Z-score of the forward return over the training window is:

\begin{equation}
    Z_{30,t} = \frac{R_{30,t} - \bar{R}_{T_{\mathrm{train}}}}{\sigma_{R, T_{\mathrm{train}}}}.
    \label{eq:zscore}
\end{equation}

To bound targets smoothly inside $(0,100)$, we apply:

\begin{equation}
    Y_{\mathrm{target},t} = 50.0 \left[ 1 + \tanh\left( \frac{15.0}{50.0} Z_{30,t} \right) \right].
    \label{eq:bounded_target}
\end{equation}

ANFIS uses a two-pass hybrid learning paradigm:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Forward pass (consequent parameters):} Consequent parameters $\boldsymbol{\beta}$ (12 parameters across 3 rules) are solved by ridge regression over $\mathbf{A}\boldsymbol{\beta} = \mathbf{Y}_{\mathrm{target}}$, where row $t$ of $\mathbf{A}$ contains $\bar{w}_{k,t} \cdot [1, \mathbf{x}_t^T]$:
    \begin{equation}
        \boldsymbol{\beta}^* = (\mathbf{A}^T \mathbf{A}+\lambda I)^{-1} \mathbf{A}^T \mathbf{Y}_{\mathrm{target}},\quad \lambda=100.
        \label{eq:ls}
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Backward pass (antecedent parameters):} Antecedent centers $c_{i,k}$ (9 parameters) are initialized and fixed by subtractive clustering, while antecedent Gaussian widths $\sigma_{i,k}$ (9 free trainable parameters) are updated by gradient descent on the squared-error objective
    \begin{equation}
        E = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{t} \left( Y_{\mathrm{raw},t} - Y_{\mathrm{target},t} \right)^2,
        \label{eq:train_error}
    \end{equation}
    with learning rate $\eta = 10^{-3}$ for 200 epochs.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Pre-AGM Rule-Weight Masking Mechanism}\label{subsec3_4}

Within the window $t \in [t_{\mathrm{AGM}} - M_{\mathrm{Pre}}, t_{\mathrm{AGM}} - 1]$ ($M_{\mathrm{Pre}} = 45$), we inject a constrained perturbation into firing strengths:

\begin{equation}
    w_{k,t}^{\text{masked}} = w_{k,t} \cdot (1 + \xi_{k,t}),
    \label{eq:masked_weight}
\end{equation}
where $\xi_{k,t}$ is sampled from a zero-mean Gaussian $\mathcal{N}\!\left(0, (\alpha/2)^2\right)$ truncated to $[-\alpha, +\alpha]$ with noise scale $\alpha = 0.05$. The truncation sits at exactly $\pm 2$ standard deviations for every $\alpha$, so the \emph{realised} variance of the draw is $\sigma_\xi^2 = \kappa_{\mathrm{trunc}}\,(\alpha/2)^2$ with retained-variance factor $\kappa_{\mathrm{trunc}} = 1 - 2a\phi(a)/(2\Phi(a)-1)\big|_{a=2} = 0.7738$; every variance statement below uses this realised variance, and the adaptive inversion of Section~\ref{subsubsec3_4_2} is calibrated to it (an uncalibrated inversion understates the noise SD by $\sqrt{\kappa_{\mathrm{trunc}}} = 0.88$, the shortfall measured in \texttt{experiments/truncation\_correction.py}).

The resulting masked crisp output before clipping is given by:

\begin{equation}
    Y_{\mathrm{raw},t}^{\text{masked}} = \frac{\sum_{k=1}^{K} w_{k,t}^{\text{masked}} \, y_{k,t}}{\sum_{k=1}^{K} w_{k,t}^{\text{masked}}},
    \label{eq:defuzz_masked}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Bias expansion with remainder]\label{prop:bias}
Assume $\{\xi_{k,t}\}_{k=1}^K$ are independent, mean-zero, with realised variance $\sigma_\xi^2 = \kappa_{\mathrm{trunc}}\,(\alpha/2)^2$ and support $[-\alpha, \alpha]$ with $\alpha < 1$ (so the masked denominator is bounded away from zero), and $|y_{k,t} - Y_{\mathrm{raw},t}| \le C$. Then the masking bias admits the second-order expansion
\begin{equation}
    \mathbb{E}\bigl[Y_{\mathrm{raw},t}^{\text{masked}}\bigr] = Y_{\mathrm{raw},t} - \sigma_\xi^2 \sum_{k=1}^{K} \bar{w}_{k,t}^2 \left( y_{k,t} - Y_{\mathrm{raw},t} \right) + R_t,
    \label{eq:bias_expansion}
\end{equation}
with remainder bounded by $|R_t| \le C\,\alpha^3/(1-\alpha)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $u_t = \sum_k \bar{w}_{k,t}\xi_{k,t}$ and $D_t = \sum_k \bar{w}_{k,t}\xi_{k,t}(y_{k,t} - Y_{\mathrm{raw},t})$. Dividing numerator and denominator of Eq.~\eqref{eq:defuzz_masked} by $\sum_k w_{k,t}$ gives the exact identity $Y^{\text{masked}}_{\mathrm{raw},t} - Y_{\mathrm{raw},t} = D_t/(1 + u_t)$. Expanding $1/(1+u_t) = 1 - u_t + u_t^2/(1+u_t)$ and taking expectations: $\mathbb{E}[D_t] = 0$ by independence and zero mean; $\mathbb{E}[D_t u_t] = \sigma_\xi^2 \sum_k \bar{w}_{k,t}^2 (y_{k,t} - Y_{\mathrm{raw},t})$; and $|R_t| = \bigl|\mathbb{E}\bigl[D_t u_t^2/(1+u_t)\bigr]\bigr| \le C\,\alpha^3/(1-\alpha)$, using $|D_t| \le C\alpha$, $|u_t| \le \alpha$. At the deployed fixed scale ($\alpha = 0.05$) the remainder bound is $0.013$ score points against a second-order term bounded by $\kappa_{\mathrm{trunc}} C (\alpha/2)^2 = 0.048$, and the realised bias ($0.006$ points, mean absolute over rows and $400$ masks) is below Monte-Carlo resolution (SE $0.007$). At the adaptive scales of Section~\ref{subsubsec3_4_2} ($\alpha_t$ up to $1.0$) the analytic remainder bound is vacuous, so the expansion is checked empirically rather than guaranteed: Monte-Carlo over $400$ masks on the Pre-AGM panel gives empirical/predicted bias ratios $0.77$, $0.89$, $1.16$ at $\alpha = 0.3$, $0.6$, $1.0$, with worst-case residual $1.09$ points at $\alpha = 1.0$. These ratios are descriptive observations without Monte-Carlo standard errors and do not establish monotone accuracy at the cap (\texttt{experiments/truncation\_correction.py}).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Masking variance identity]\label{thm:masking}
Under the assumptions of Proposition~\ref{prop:bias}, define the normalised firing weights $\bar{w}_{k,t} = w_{k,t}/\sum_j w_{j,t}$, the effective number of active rules $K_{\mathrm{eff},t} = 1/\sum_k \bar{w}_{k,t}^2$ (inverse Herfindahl), and the weighted consequent dispersion $V_t = \sum_k \bar{w}_{k,t}(y_{k,t} - Y_{\mathrm{raw},t})^2$. Then the masked-output variance satisfies, to second order in $\sigma_\xi$ (the delta-method expansion of the ratio in Eq.~\eqref{eq:defuzz_masked}; odd moments vanish by the symmetry of the truncated draw):
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{Var}\bigl[Y_{\mathrm{raw},t}^{\text{masked}}\bigr] = \sigma_\xi^2 \underbrace{\sum_{k=1}^{K} \bar{w}_{k,t}^2 \bigl(y_{k,t} - Y_{\mathrm{raw},t}\bigr)^2}_{S_t} + O(\sigma_\xi^4).
    \label{eq:masking_variance}
\end{equation}
The injected variance is governed by the dispersion scale $S_t$ itself. Two structural corollaries follow. First, writing the masking amplification factor $\Lambda_t = \sum_k \bar{w}_{k,t}^2 (y_{k,t} - Y_{\mathrm{raw},t})^2 / V_t$, so that $\mathrm{Var}[Y^{\text{masked}}_t] = \sigma_\xi^2 \Lambda_t V_t$: for uniform weights ($\bar{w}_k = 1/K$) $\Lambda_t = 1/K$, while in the limit of a single dominant rule ($\bar{w}_1 \to 1$) $\Lambda_t \to 0$ and masking vanishes --- at most one rule's deviation survives renormalisation, multiplied by the vanishing weight of every other rule. Second, since $\sum_k \bar{w}_{k,t}^2 = 1/K_{\mathrm{eff},t}$ exactly,
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{Var}\bigl[Y_{\mathrm{raw},t}^{\text{masked}}\bigr] \le \sigma_\xi^2 \cdot \max_k \bigl(y_{k,t} - Y_{\mathrm{raw},t}\bigr)^2 \cdot \frac{1}{K_{\mathrm{eff},t}},
    \label{eq:masking_bound}
\end{equation}
which is an \emph{upper} bound only, and must be read as such: a small $K_{\mathrm{eff},t}$ caps how much variance the mask \emph{can} inject but does not by itself make that variance small --- a low-weight rule with a large consequent deviation (up to $C$) can still dominate $S_t$ --- so efficacy at any given row is governed by the realised dispersion $S_t$ (together with the noise scale $\sigma_\xi^2$), not by $K_{\mathrm{eff},t}$ alone.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Write $w_k^{\text{masked}} = w_k(1+\xi_k)$ and expand $Y^{\text{masked}} = \sum_k w_k(1+\xi_k)y_k / \sum_j w_j(1+\xi_j)$ to first order in $\xi$: the denominator contributes $-\sum_j \bar{w}_j \xi_j \cdot Y_{\mathrm{raw}}$, giving $Y^{\text{masked}}_t - Y_{\mathrm{raw},t} \approx \sum_k \bar{w}_{k,t}\xi_{k,t}(y_{k,t} - Y_{\mathrm{raw},t})$. Independence of the $\xi_{k,t}$ yields Eq.~\eqref{eq:masking_variance} directly. For the regimes: at uniform weights $\bar{w}_k^2 = 1/K^2$ factors out and the sum is $V_t/K$; at concentration $\bar{w}_1 \to 1$ every term carries a factor $\bar{w}_k^2 (y_k - Y_{\mathrm{raw}})^2$ in which either $\bar{w}_k \to 0$ ($k > 1$) or $(y_1 - Y_{\mathrm{raw}}) \to 0$ ($k = 1$), so the sum vanishes. The bound follows from factoring $\max_k (y_k - Y_{\mathrm{raw}})^2$ out of the sum and recognising the remainder as $1/K_{\mathrm{eff},t}$ exactly --- a one-directional cap, not an equivalence. On the paper's Pre-AGM panel the truncation-calibrated first-order prediction matches Monte-Carlo masked-output variance with cross-row correlation $0.999$ and median empirical/predicted ratio $0.99$--$1.01$ for $\alpha \le 0.2$ ($1.25$ at $\alpha = 1.0$, the second-order correction; without the $\kappa$ calibration the ratio is $0.77$); \texttt{experiments/adaptive\_masking.py} and \texttt{experiments/truncation\_correction.py}.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Dispersion-Adaptive Masking}\label{subsubsec3_4_2}
Proposition~\ref{thm:masking} is constructive: because the first-order variance is known in closed form, the noise scale can be inverted per observation so the masked output carries a target variance $\epsilon_{\mathrm{target}}^2$ wherever the non-negativity cap below does not bind --- with the truncation factor $\kappa_{\mathrm{trunc}}$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:masked_weight} restored, since inverting the uncalibrated identity would undershoot the target by $\sqrt{\kappa_{\mathrm{trunc}}}$:
\begin{equation}
    \alpha_t = \min\!\left(\alpha_{\max},\, \frac{2\epsilon_{\mathrm{target}}}{\sqrt{\kappa_{\mathrm{trunc}}\,S_t}}\right), \quad S_t = \sum_{k=1}^{K} \bar{w}_{k,t}^2 \bigl(y_{k,t} - Y_{\mathrm{raw},t}\bigr)^2.
    \label{eq:adaptive_alpha}
\end{equation}
Calibration is not cosmetic: the uncalibrated inversion $2\epsilon_{\mathrm{target}}/\sqrt{S_t}$ realises only $\sqrt{\kappa_{\mathrm{trunc}}}\,\epsilon_{\mathrm{target}} \approx 0.88\epsilon_{\mathrm{target}}$ even on uncapped rows ($1.76$ against the $2.0$ target on the Pre-AGM panel), whereas Eq.~\eqref{eq:adaptive_alpha} realises $1.04\,\epsilon_{\mathrm{target}}$ there --- the residual $+4\%$ is the second-order variance correction measured in Proposition~\ref{prop:bias}'s Monte-Carlo check ($1.065$ empirical/predicted at $\alpha = 0.6$). Where the cap $\alpha_{\max}$ binds, the target noise is not reached; the binding rate is reported alongside the result. Because $\alpha_t$ grows as $S_t$ shrinks, the cap binds on the rows with the \emph{smallest} consequent dispersion --- where even $\alpha_{\max}$ cannot lift the noise to $\epsilon_{\mathrm{target}}$ --- not on the noisiest rows. Equation~\eqref{eq:adaptive_alpha} is guarded against the degenerate case: when $S_t$ is numerically zero ($S_t \le 10^{-8}$) --- i.e., all rules agree on the consequent, $y_{k,t} = Y_{\mathrm{raw},t}$ --- the score is invariant to rule-weight masking and $\alpha_t$ is set to zero rather than dividing by zero. The cap is defined as the largest noise scale preserving non-negative masked weights,
\begin{equation}
    \alpha_{\max} = \max\bigl\{\alpha \ge 0 : w_{k,t}(1+\xi) \ge 0 \ \ \forall k,\ \forall |\xi| \le \alpha\bigr\} = 1.0,
    \label{eq:alpha_max}
\end{equation}
since $w_{k,t}^{\text{masked}} = w_{k,t}(1 + \xi_{k,t})$ with $|\xi_{k,t}| \le \alpha_t$: any $\alpha_t > 1$ would permit sign-reversing weights and break the convexity of the defuzzification sum. Relaxing this constraint would require a defuzzification scheme tolerating signed weights (Section~\ref{sec6}). Bias control is preserved: Proposition~\ref{prop:bias} applies row-wise with the realised variance $\sigma_{\xi,t}^2 = \kappa_{\mathrm{trunc}}\,(\alpha_t/2)^2$, so the adaptive scheme trades a larger (but still bounded and reported) bias for a calibrated noise target --- the bias--efficacy frontier the fixed scheme cannot reach. The implementation is \texttt{src/masking.py:adaptive\_masked\_firing\_scores}.

\begin{remark}[Threat-model scope]\label{rem:threat_model}
Proposition~\ref{prop:bias} bounds only the bias of the masked output. It does not establish query-averaging robustness: an adversary who can query the model $Q$ times for the same input vector $\mathbf{x}_t$ and average the responses observes an effective noise standard deviation of $\sigma_\xi / \sqrt{Q}$. At the deployed $\alpha = 0.05$ (realised $\sigma_\xi = \sqrt{\kappa_{\mathrm{trunc}}} \cdot 0.025 = 0.022$, $C = 100$) a single query leaves up to $2.2$ score points of noise, about $20$ averaged queries bring the error below $0.5$ points, and about $480$ queries below $0.1$ points.\footnote{The query-budget arithmetic is implemented in \texttt{src/masking.py} (\texttt{unlimited\_query\_bound}).} The masking is therefore \emph{obfuscation with a quantified query budget}, not security against an unlimited-query adversary --- consistent with the model-extraction literature, where repeated API queries (including averaging over noisy outputs) recover target models with high fidelity \cite{tramer2016} --- and production use requires pairing it with rate-limiting, query authentication, and randomized execution schedules (Section~\ref{subsec5_2}).
\end{remark}

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{TSK inference with CLV directional mode and dispersion-adaptive Pre-AGM masking}\label{alg:tsk_eval}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Bar row $\{P_t,H_t,L_t,V_t\}$, mode \texttt{clv\_mode}, parameters $\{c_{i,k},\sigma_{i,k},\boldsymbol{\beta}_k\}_{k=1}^K$, indicator $I_{\mathrm{PreAGM}} \in \{0,1\}$, masking scheme $\texttt{scheme} \in \{\texttt{adaptive},\texttt{fixed}\}$, target noise $\epsilon_{\mathrm{target}} = 2.0$, cap $\alpha_{\max} = 1.0$, fixed-scale rate $\alpha = 0.05$, truncation factor $\kappa_{\mathrm{trunc}} = 0.7738$.
\Ensure Score $Y_{\mathrm{attr},t} \in [0,100]$.
\State Compute $Dev_{\mathrm{Price},t}$ and $Dev_{\mathrm{Volume},t}$ via Eq.~\eqref{eq:devprice}--\eqref{eq:devvol}.
\If{\texttt{clv\_mode='clv'} \textbf{or} (\texttt{clv\_mode='hybrid'} \textbf{and} $H_t, L_t$ exist \textbf{and} $H_t > L_t$)}
    \State $p_t \leftarrow \dfrac{2 P_t - H_t - L_t}{H_t - L_t + \epsilon_{\mathrm{reg}}}$
\Else \Comment{Tick rule, also the hybrid fallback when bounds are missing}
    \State $p_t \leftarrow \mathrm{sgn}(P_t - P_{t-1})$
\EndIf
\State $\mathbf{x}_t \leftarrow [Dev_{\mathrm{Price},t}, Dev_{\mathrm{Volume},t}, p_t]^T$.
\For{$k = 1$ \textbf{to} $K$}
    \State Compute $\mu_{i,k}(x_{i,t})$ via Eq.~\eqref{eq:gauss_mf}
    \State $w_{k,t} \leftarrow \prod_{i=1}^{3} \mu_{i,k}(x_{i,t})$
    \State $y_{k,t} \leftarrow \beta_{0,k} + \sum_{i=1}^3 \beta_{i,k} x_{i,t}$
\EndFor
\State $Y_{\mathrm{raw},t} \leftarrow \sum_k \bar{w}_{k,t} y_{k,t}$ with $\bar{w}_{k,t} = w_{k,t}/\sum_j w_{j,t}$
\If{$I_{\mathrm{PreAGM}} = 1$}
    \If{\texttt{scheme} = \texttt{adaptive}}
        \State $S_t \leftarrow \sum_k \bar{w}_{k,t}^2 (y_{k,t} - Y_{\mathrm{raw},t})^2$ \Comment{Proposition~\ref{thm:masking}}
        \If{$S_t \le 10^{-8}$} \Comment{all rules agree; score invariant}
            \State $\alpha_t \leftarrow 0$
        \Else
            \State $\alpha_t \leftarrow \min(\alpha_{\max},\, 2\epsilon_{\mathrm{target}}/\sqrt{\kappa_{\mathrm{trunc}} S_t})$ \Comment{Eq.~\eqref{eq:adaptive_alpha}}
        \EndIf
    \Else \Comment{fixed-scale fallback}
        \State $\alpha_t \leftarrow 0.05$
    \EndIf
    \For{$k = 1$ \textbf{to} $K$}
        \State Sample $\xi_{k,t} \sim \mathcal{N}\left(0, (\alpha_t/2)^2\right)$ truncated to $[-\alpha_t, +\alpha_t]$
        \State $w_{k,t} \leftarrow w_{k,t} (1 + \xi_{k,t})$
    \EndFor
\EndIf
\State $S_w \leftarrow \sum_{k=1}^{K} w_{k,t}$
\If{$S_w < 10^{-8}$}
    \State \Return $50.0$
\EndIf
\State \Return $\min\bigl(100.0, \max(0.0, \sum_k w_{k,t} y_{k,t} / S_w)\bigr)$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Experimental Setup and Financial Data}\label{subsec4_1}

We integrate three data streams from the Kazakhstan Stock Exchange (KASE) and the National Bank of Kazakhstan (NBK), all ingested from raw exchange/broker exports:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Daily market bars:} OHLC and volume for seven KASE equities (AIRA, KCEL, KEGC, KMGZ, KZAP, KZTK, KZTO), covering 2022-01-05 to 2025-12-31 ($6{,}140$ bars in total: $983$--$984$ for KCEL, KEGC, KZAP, KZTK and KZTO, $756$ for KMGZ which lists from 2022-12-09, and $467$ for AIRA which lists from 2024-02-12).
    \item \textbf{Macroeconomic rates:} Daily TONIA repo series ($986$ observations over the same span, mean $14.96\%$ p.a.) driving the risk-free adjustment of Eq.~\eqref{eq:fwd_return}, plus the NBK daily USD/KZT reference rate as a contextual macro series (not an input to the three-input engine).
    \item \textbf{Corporate disclosures:} Audited EBITDA, net debt, and free cash flow to equity (FCFE) in mln KZT, supplied per reporting year and per reporting period (quarterly and half-year legs, plus annual \mbox{``for the year''} totals where published).
\end{enumerate}

The model is trained on 2022-01-01 to 2024-12-31 ($4{,}278$ pooled asset-days over $718$ trading days --- $718$ each for KCEL and KEGC, $717$ for KZAP, KZTK and KZTO, $490$ for KMGZ and $201$ for AIRA, whose histories begin later --- the window start being trimmed to 2022-02-07 by the feature warm-up) and evaluated strictly out-of-sample on the complete 2025 calendar year ($1{,}722$ asset-days over $246$ trading days per equity), of which $1{,}512$ carry an observable 30-day forward return ($216$ per equity; the final month of 2025 lacks a forward label). The payout mapping is retained as an exploratory, unpurged application of the deployed $K=5$, $r=0.5$, $\lambda=10$ score, not the frozen $K=3$ primary model, and is not evidence of predictive or decision-alignment skill. Model output maps to corporate Payout Ratio $PR_t \in [0\%, 100\%]$ via

\begin{equation}
    PR_{\mathrm{target},t} = \psi(Y_{\mathrm{attr},t}) \cdot \Phi\!\left(\frac{\mathrm{Net\ Debt}}{\mathrm{EBITDA}}, \mathrm{FCFE}\right),
    \label{eq:payout}
\end{equation}
where $\psi(Y) = \max\bigl(0, \min(1, Y/100)\bigr)$ and the leverage/FCFE penalty function $\Phi$ is defined as:

\begin{equation}
    \Phi(L, F) =
    \begin{cases}
        \dfrac{1}{1 + \exp\!\bigl(\kappa_{\mathrm{lev}} (L - \tau)\bigr)}, & F > 0, \\[6pt]
        0, & F \le 0,
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:phi}
\end{equation}
with leverage penalty intensity $\kappa_{\mathrm{lev}} = 3.0$ and Net Debt/EBITDA threshold $\tau = 1.0$. Both are set a priori, not tuned: $\tau = 1.0$ is a conventional investment-grade leverage screen, and $\kappa_{\mathrm{lev}} = 3.0$ makes the penalty decisive ($1/(1+e^{3}) \approx 0.05$ at $L = 2.0$) by construction, so the mapping is deliberately conservative --- it screens out leveraged payers rather than pricing their distress finely. The KEGC and KZTO rows of Table~\ref{tab:h1_validation} show the cost of that conservatism directly.

\noindent\textbf{Portfolio construction algorithm.} For completeness, the reported top-3 portfolio is a long-only, equal-weighted portfolio, not a score-weighted portfolio. At the first available trading day of each calendar month, the algorithm filters to names with a non-missing $R_{30,t}$ label, sorts them by the frozen score $Y_{\mathrm{attr},t}$ in descending order, and selects the three highest-ranked names. Exact score ties are resolved deterministically by ascending ticker symbol. Each selected name receives weight $1/3$ and is held for the following 30 trading days represented by $R_{30,t}$; the return therefore starts after the day-$t$ observation and uses the close-to-close interval from $P_t$ to $P_{t+30}$, with dividends and the risk-free adjustment defined in Eq.~\eqref{eq:fwd_return}. The score uses the day-$t$ close, but no same-close executable fill is assumed. The portfolio return for month $m$ is
\[
R^{\mathrm{port}}_m = \frac{1}{3}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{P}_m}R_{30,i}.
\]
For a cost tier of $c$ basis points per round trip, month-to-month membership turnover is computed from the symmetric difference of consecutive three-name sets:
\[
\tau_m = \frac{|\mathcal{P}_m\triangle\mathcal{P}_{m-1}|}{2\cdot 3}.
\]
The reported annualised turnover is $12$ times the mean $\tau_m$, and the monthly cost deduction is $(\overline{\tau}\,c/10{,}000)$ before annualising the net monthly Sharpe by $\sqrt{12}$. Thus costs are applied as a proportional turnover haircut; there is no spread, market-impact, leverage, or intramonth execution model. Months with fewer than three labelled names are omitted. The implementation is \texttt{src/backtest.py:top\_n\_portfolio\_returns}.

\subsection{Rule-Concentration Diagnostic and Dispersion-Adaptive Masking Evaluation}\label{subsec4_4}

\noindent\textbf{Adversarial Reconstruction Protocol:} To evaluate masking efficacy against model reconstruction, we model an external party attempting to reconstruct internal scoring logic by fitting a Support Vector Regressor (SVR with RBF kernel, $C=1.0$, $\gamma=\text{'scale'}$) on observable market inputs $\mathbf{x}_t$. Because the masking of Section~\ref{subsec3_4} is armed only inside the Pre-AGM windows, the adversary is restricted to exactly those observations: it is trained on the 2024 Pre-AGM panel ($N = 205$ asset-days) and evaluated out-of-sample on the 2025 Pre-AGM panel ($N = 208$ asset-days). All results in this subsection use the frozen primary $K=3$ model; the test-informed deployed model is reported only as a sensitivity. The adversary operates under a strict single-query budget: $Q = 1$ observation vector per trading day per equity. This is the most favourable case for the defender: as Remark~\ref{rem:threat_model} establishes, an adversary with unlimited queries ($Q \gg 1$) can average out independently redrawn masking noise, so the $Q=1$ protocol bounds masking efficacy from above rather than characterising it. Critically, every $R^2$ below is scored against the \emph{unmasked} test scores (fidelity to the clean model), never against masked labels. The reported values are conditional single-panel, single-seed diagnostics; they are not confidence intervals over the 208 rows. The adversary is weak by construction: untuned, trained on $205$ rows, and denied the unmasked history outside the armed windows. No link is claimed between reconstruction $R^2$ and any trading loss: the protected object is the rule base as intellectual property (model-extraction defence). The adversary recovers $R^2 = 0.533$ when trained on unmasked scores. \emph{Fixed masking does not reduce this:} the same-seed adversary trained on $\alpha=0.05$ scores attains $R^2=0.530$.

\noindent\textbf{Rule-concentration diagnostic:} Proposition~\ref{thm:masking} frames the fixed-masking null reported above, but does not predict it on its own. On the frozen 2025 Pre-AGM panel ($N=208$), the median effective rule count is $K_{\mathrm{eff}}=1.40$ (mean $1.66$, max $3.00$ of $K=3$), with median amplification $\Lambda=0.13$. The variance identity validates cleanly: across $\alpha \in \{0.01,0.05,0.10,0.20\}$ the truncation-calibrated prediction matches Monte-Carlo masked-output variance with cross-row correlation $0.997$--$0.998$ and median empirical/predicted ratios $0.995$--$1.020$ at every scale. The deployed $K=5$ values are retained only as a sensitivity and are not substituted into the frozen result.

\noindent\textbf{Adaptive masking result (fidelity, not dilution):} Inverting Proposition~\ref{thm:masking} per observation at target noise $\epsilon_{\mathrm{target}}=2.0$, with $\alpha_{\max}=1.0$, gives median $\alpha_t=1.00$ and a $77.2\%$ cap-binding rate on the frozen $K=3$ model. Scored against the clean test scores, the same SVR adversary attains fidelity $R^2=0.377$ on the seeded adaptive draw, down from $0.533$ unmasked and $0.530$ fixed-masked. Keyed fixed masking at $\alpha=1.0$ gives $R^2=0.453$ for $Q=1$ and the same $0.453$ for repeated $Q=25$ responses. These are descriptive single-seed results, not sampling confidence intervals or security guarantees; the adaptive signed bias is $-0.313$ points and its mean absolute perturbation is $0.972$ points in this run. The frozen-model audit therefore supplies the adversarial fidelity that the earlier variance-only transfer check lacked, while retaining the caveats about panel dependence, query averaging, and adaptive-scale leakage.

\noindent\textbf{Sensitivity scope:} The earlier deployed $K=5$, test-informed audit remains available as a sensitivity analysis only. Its materially different concentration and cap regime must not be read as evidence for the frozen protocol.

\noindent\textbf{Cap-binding regime:} The calibrated target is reachable only on the $22.8\%$ of frozen-model rows where $\alpha_t < \alpha_{\max}$; on the $77.2\%$ of rows where $\alpha_t$ hits $\alpha_{\max}=1.0$, the target is unreachable. Because $\alpha_t=2\epsilon_{\mathrm{target}}/\sqrt{\kappa_{\mathrm{trunc}} S_t}$ grows as $S_t$ shrinks, the cap binds precisely on rows of smallest consequent dispersion --- the concentrated-firing rows. This describes injected noise, not protection. The cap rate is reported with every result so the achieved rather than targeted noise floor remains visible. Per-tercile figures below are descriptive; their non-monotone ordering is not evidence of a statistically established adaptive advantage.

\noindent\textbf{Matched-noise comparison (fidelity):} In the frozen audit, fixed masking at $\alpha=1.0$ realises noise SD $1.54$ and attains fidelity $R^2=0.338$, compared with adaptive's $0.377$ on the same seeded protocol. This comparison is descriptive and conditional on one serially dependent panel and mask draws; without a joint row/date bootstrap it does not establish an adaptive advantage. The earlier deployed matched-noise comparison is not part of the frozen primary result.

\noindent\textbf{Per-row reconstruction error (concentrated rows):} The frozen audit also reports tercile values descriptively over roughly $70$ rows in the lowest $S_t$ tercile; these single-draw values are not inferential evidence. In that tercile, unmasked, fixed, matched-fixed, and adaptive fidelity are respectively $0.439$, $0.450$, $0.478$, and $0.501$. The masked values exceeding the unmasked value are consistent with sampling and fit noise; no per-row advantage is claimed. The deployed-model tercile table is retained only as a sensitivity artifact and is not used for the frozen protocol conclusion.

\noindent\textbf{Noise--efficacy trade-off:} In the frozen audit, adaptive $\epsilon_{\mathrm{target}}=2.0$ has signed bias $-0.313$ points and mean absolute perturbation $0.972$ points in the seeded run. These are a noise-cost diagnostic and not a bias estimate with sampling uncertainty. No multi-seed frontier is claimed for the frozen model here.

\noindent\textbf{Query-budget averaging (Remark~\ref{rem:threat_model}):} For the frozen model, keyed fixed masking with a server-side secret and asset/date key gives fidelity $R^2=0.453$ for $Q=1$ and remains $0.453$ for repeated $Q=25$ responses because the same key returns the same value. This is a measured single-key result, not a security guarantee: key compromise, cross-key observations, and adaptive-scale leakage remain outside scope. The in-window restriction assumes an authenticated client can query scores only during the 30 trading days before an AGM, while clean scores outside that window are observable to a separate public or internal history; without that access asymmetry, the in-window experiment is not the relevant threat model.

\noindent\textbf{Exploratory sensitivity frontier:} The earlier multi-draw $R^2$--noise frontier was generated for the deployed, test-informed $K=5$ model. It is not evidence for the frozen protocol and is omitted from the frozen quantitative conclusion.

\noindent\textbf{Attacker-strength limitation:} Strong-extractor and clean out-of-window results currently belong to the deployed sensitivity audit, not the frozen primary model. They therefore establish only that the deployed defense can be overturned, not a measured upper bound for the frozen scorer. The frozen result is deliberately limited to the untuned in-window SVR and keyed repeated-query check reported above.

\section{Discussion and Practical Implications}\label{sec5}

\subsection{Interpretation in Small Sample Regimes}\label{subsec5_1}

Under the frozen protocol the scorer has 21 free trainable parameters and a 15-bps net Sharpe of $0.98$; the test-informed deployed setting is higher ($1.94$) but is reported only as sensitivity. Neither estimate establishes forecasting skill: the frozen monthly cross-sectional IC is $0.201$ with a date-clustered 95\% interval of $[-0.013,0.432]$, and the pooled asset-day IC is $0.052$ with interval $[-0.010,0.131]$. The three-fold walk-forward mean is $0.161$ with a $t$-interval of $[-0.015,0.338]$ for IC and $0.65$ with $[-0.266,1.569]$ for Sharpe. The supplied KZTK $26{,}664.88$~KZT/share special-dividend record date is 2025-05-25 (a Sunday), not a verified exchange ex-date; it must not be described as an ex-date until the source field is corrected. Labels are dividend-adjusted throughout (\texttt{data/raw/financials/dividends.csv}: 26 paid rows; cancelled declarations excluded).

\subsection{Deployment Considerations: Defense-in-Depth Framing}\label{subsec5_2}

The stochastic masking mechanism should be understood as a defense-in-depth measure for model-extraction friction, not front-running protection or an isolated security guarantee. The Section~\ref{subsec4_4} audit shows that $Q = 25$ independent queries restore in-window fidelity and clean out-of-window history recovers the static model under every independently redrawn scheme. A keyed response prevents averaging only for the same key, as the keyed audit demonstrates; it does not solve key compromise or leakage through adaptive noise scale. Production use therefore requires an explicit observability policy, authentication, rate-limiting, and query-pattern detection; no trading-loss claim is made.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec6}

The primary contribution is a transparent masking variance identity and its calibrated, row-wise implementation, not a demonstrated defense or finance signal. On the frozen primary model, the seeded in-window SVR fidelity is $0.533$ without masking, $0.530$ with fixed $\alpha=0.05$, and $0.377$ with adaptive masking at $\epsilon=2.0$; keyed repeated-query fidelity is $0.453$ for both $Q=1$ and $Q=25$. These conditional diagnostics show limited extraction friction rather than a security guarantee, and the deployed test-informed audit is only a sensitivity. After freezing all 36 hyperparameter trials on pre-2025 data and applying a 30-trading-day embargo, the 2025 frozen specification gives monthly IC $0.201$ (date-clustered 95\% CI $[-0.013,0.432]$) and net Sharpe $0.983$ at 15 bps, falling to $0.737$ at 150 bps. The trial ledger reports validation IC, RMSE, MAE, $C$, and loss; it does not support a proper trial-level DSR, so no 36-trial DSR is claimed. The supplied dividend file is used for labels, but its KZTK 2025-05-25 field is a Sunday record date rather than a verified exchange ex-date; payout comparisons also mix percent-of-profit actuals with percent-of-FCFE targets. These limitations, the small number of monthly rebalances, and the absence of a validated finance or security advantage define this as a negative protocol result.
